<b>Full-Scale Conversion of Wastewater Treatment Plant from Biological to Chemical Phosphorus Removal for Vivianite Formation</b>

Ha Nguyen, Leon Korving, Thomas Prot, Damien Cazalet, H. Pieter J. van Veelen, Samarpita Roy, Carlo Belloni, A. Iulian. Dugulan and Mark C.M. van Loosdrecht

<br>[paper]() published in XXX (XXXX);XXX:XXXXXXX
<br>

This study evaluates the operational impacts and recovery potential of converting a full-scale wastewater treatment plant from Enhanced Biological Phosphorus Removal (EBPR) to Chemical Phosphorus Removal (CPR) with the aim to obtain phosphate recovery in the form of vivianite. By increasing iron dosing to an Fe/P molar ratio from 0.5 to 1.6 in digested sludge, up to 90 % of sludge phosphorus was bound as vivianite. Unexpectedly, significant vivianite formation may have occurred mostly in the secondary system, rather than being confined to the anaerobic digester. The transition to CPR improved effluent quality (86 % lower P and 36 % lower COD), reduced biogas H₂S levels from 332 ppm to 1 ppm, and increased dewatered cake dry matter from 24 % to 30 %. Mass balances indicated that dosing iron into the primary thickener triggered substantial reduction of Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺, causing iron recirculation to the influent and possibly impairing primary settling. Microbial analysis showed pronounced but reversible shifts in community structure, with Polyphosphate-Accumulating Organisms (PAOs) recovering after the return to EBPR operation. These results show that converting EBPR plants to CPR can substantially increase vivianite formation in sludge as a preparation step for downstream vivianite recovery. For future implementation, maintaining waterline iron dosing for effluent P control while dosing the additional iron required for vivianite formation directly to the digester is recommended to improve process stability.
